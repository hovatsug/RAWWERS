# Your introduction video: a one-minute guide

For photographers on RAWWERS. It's optional, it takes about ten minutes, and you can replace or remove it any time.

## Why it's worth a minute of your time

- **Clients hire a person, not just photos.** Your portfolio shows what you make. A minute of you shows what it's like to be photographed by you, and that is often what decides who gets the booking.
- **It builds trust before the first message.** Handing a wedding, a family session or a product launch to a stranger feels risky. Seeing and hearing you makes it feel less so.
- **It helps if you're new.** Reviews take time to collect. A clear, honest introduction lets clients get to know you now.
- **It saves you time.** When clients already know your style and how you work, the requests you get come from people who fit what you do.

## The one-minute shape

Aim for about 100 to 130 words. A normal speaking pace gets you to about a minute, and 30 to 45 seconds works well too. Five short parts:

1. **Who you are** (about 5 seconds). Your first name, what you shoot, where.
2. **Your style** (about 15 seconds). Who you work with, and one plain sentence about how your photos look and feel.
3. **What it's like to work with you** (about 20 seconds). Before, during and after the shoot. This is the part only you can say.
4. **One true detail** (about 10 seconds). Your experience, your training, or why you started. If you're new, say so. Honesty works.
5. **An invitation** (about 10 seconds). Tell them to look at your packages and send you a request.

### Fill-in template

"Hi, I'm [first name], a [what you shoot] photographer in [city].
I work with [who you work with], and my photos are [your style, in plain words].
When you book me, [what the experience is like for them].
[One true detail: your years, your training, or why you started.]
Take a look at my packages and send me a request. I'd love to hear about it."

## Two examples to adapt (don't copy them)

**An experienced wedding photographer**

"Hi, I'm Marco, a wedding photographer in Lisbon. I photograph weddings the way they really happen: no stiff poses, no ordering your guests around. I stay close and quiet, and I wait for the moments you'd otherwise miss, like your dad's face when you walk in, or the laugh between the vows. Before the day, we meet for a coffee, so I'm already a friendly face and not a stranger with a camera. I've photographed sixty weddings in six years, and I deliver every gallery within three weeks. If that's the feeling you want, take a look at my packages and send me a request. I'd love to hear your story."

**Someone brand new**

"Hi, I'm Ana, a portrait photographer in London, and I'm new here. I'll be honest about what I offer: I take my time. Most people tell me they hate being photographed, so we start by chatting, I show you a few frames on the back of the camera, and only then do we try poses. I trained for three years, I shoot with professional gear, and I edit every photo by hand. If you'd like portraits that feel like you, send me a request. I'd love to meet you."

Every fact in these examples is invented. Use your own, and only things that are true.

## What to talk about, by niche

- **Weddings:** how you stay calm and in the background, whether you meet the couple beforehand, how you catch real moments, and when they'll get their photos.
- **Portraits:** how you help people relax. Most people think they photograph badly. Say what you do about that.
- **Family:** how you work with children and different ages, how patient you are, and where you like to shoot.
- **Events and nightlife:** how you work in low light and fast-moving rooms, how you read the room, and how quickly you deliver.
- **Corporate:** how you keep a team looking consistent, how little time you need from busy people, and that you're punctual and discreet.
- **Food:** how you work with chefs and menus, how you approach light and styling, and what the photos will be used for.
- **Real estate:** how you make a space look bright and true to life, how many photos you deliver, and how fast an agent gets them.
- **Product:** how you keep backgrounds and lighting consistent across a range, how you handle close detail, and that the files are ready for online shops.

## Do

- Speak to one person, using "you" and not "clients".
- Be specific. "I wait for the laugh between the vows" says more than "I capture memories".
- Use short sentences, and say each idea once.
- Smile when you start and when you finish.
- Do a few takes. The third is usually the best, and it doesn't need to be perfect.

## Don't

- **Don't mention prices, dates or availability, or promise how fast you reply.** They change, and your profile already shows them. A promise you can't keep hurts you.
- **Don't share a phone number, email or social handle.** Keep conversations on RAWWERS, where payments are secure and there's a way to resolve problems.
- **Don't use music** unless you own it. Speak to the camera.
- **Don't show other people's faces** unless they've said yes, and don't use footage you didn't shoot.
- **Don't claim anything you can't back up,** like awards or numbers. Your gear and tier are already verified and shown on your profile.
- **Don't read from a script.** Write a few cue words and say it in your own voice.

## Filming tips

- Hold the phone vertically at eye level, or lean it on something so it's steady. Wipe the lens first.
- Stand facing a window so the light is on your face, not behind you.
- Find a quiet place. Clients hear your voice before they judge anything else.
- Recording stops at one minute, so a timer is shown on screen.
- Already have a video that's too long? Trim it in your Photos app first.

## Before you post

- [ ] I said one true detail.
- [ ] I didn't mention prices, dates, contacts or reply times.
- [ ] It's one minute or shorter, and I can hear my voice clearly.
